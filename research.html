Works in progress

01Family law · India

When Divorce Becomes Harder: Marriage Markets and Women’s Outcomes

With Rounak Bose

This project studies the effects of India’s 2019 ban on instant unilateral divorce (“triple talaq”) on marriage formation and household outcomes among Muslims. The reform increased the legal and social costs of divorce, raising questions about who enters marriage and the households that form afterward.

Using the National Family Health Survey and the Periodic Labour Force Survey, we examine marriage cohorts before and after the reform. The analysis considers marriage timing, spousal characteristics, education, employment, and women’s participation in household decisions. The results suggest shifts in marriage timing and household formation, alongside changes in women’s employment and decision-making.

02Property rights · Pakistan

Intended and Unintended Consequences of a De Facto Inheritance Reform

With Sabrin Beg, Erica Field, Jeremy Lebow, Kate Vyborny, and Suzanna Khalifa

This study examines a Punjab land records reform that digitized and centralized records and introduced biometric verification to reduce women’s exclusion from inheritance. We use the staggered rollout of the reform, together with variation in the timing of family deaths, to study whether stronger enforcement changes women’s de facto land rights.

The research finds that the probability of female inheritance rose from 13% to 22%. It also examines unintended responses for younger women, including changes in marriage timing, consent, schooling, and childbearing. The project considers how household resource allocation and marriage practices may help explain these outcomes.

03Gender & health · Pakistan

Child Health Consequences and Son Preference of a De Facto Inheritance Reform

This paper asks whether stronger property rights for women can affect gender gaps in children’s health where families favor sons. It studies the rollout of Punjab’s Land Records Management and Information System across districts and birth cohorts, linking reform exposure to vaccination, care-seeking, and survival.

The analysis reports worse early-life health outcomes for girls relative to boys after the reform and investigates whether changes in household resource allocation contribute to the gap. The findings highlight how the effects of legal reform may depend on prevailing social norms.

Policy writing

Education and accountability

Policy Recommendations for Advancing the Education Sector in Pakistan

Chapter 10 in Striving to Meet the Sustainable Development Goals: Next Steps for Policymakers and Practitioners, Asian Development Bank Institute · With Sabrin Beg

The chapter compares inputs and learning outcomes in Pakistan’s public and private schools. It discusses differences in amenities, teacher availability, and teacher quality, and considers how accountability mechanisms and performance incentives may help explain differences in school outcomes.
